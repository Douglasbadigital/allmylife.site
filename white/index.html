Understanding Prostate Health: What Every Man Over 50 Should Know

A clear, practical guide to common prostate problems, warning signs, risk factors, screening, and the everyday habits that support a healthy prostate.

Why This Matters

Prostate changes are one of the most common health issues for American men as they age. Many men put up with symptoms for years, thinking they're just a normal part of getting older.

About half of men in their 50s — and up to 9 in 10 men over 80 — have an enlarged prostate.

What Is the Prostate?

The prostate is a small gland, about the size of a walnut, that sits just below the bladder and wraps around the urethra — the tube that carries urine out of the body. Because of where it sits, even small changes in the prostate can affect how you urinate.

Enlarged Prostate (BPH)

A non-cancerous growth of the prostate. Very common with age. It can squeeze the urethra and make urination harder.

Prostatitis

Swelling or inflammation of the prostate. It can affect men of any age and may cause pain or discomfort.

Prostate Cancer

One of the most common cancers in American men. Often has no symptoms early on, which is why screening matters.

Common Warning Signs

Talk to your doctor if you notice any of these:

  • Waking up often at night to urinate
  • A sudden, urgent need to go
  • Trouble starting to urinate
  • A weak or slow urine stream
  • Dribbling at the end of urination
  • Feeling like your bladder isn't fully empty
  • Pain or burning during urination

Are You at Higher Risk?

Your risk of prostate problems is higher if you:

  • Are 50 years of age or older
  • Have a father or brother who had prostate problems or prostate cancer
  • Are overweight, especially around the waist
  • Are physically active less than 3 times a week
  • Have high blood pressure, diabetes, or heart disease
  • Are African American (higher risk of prostate cancer)

Know Your Screening Options

Simple checkups can tell you where you stand. These are the main tests doctors use to check the prostate:

TestWhat It ChecksWhen to Ask
PSA blood testLevels of a protein made by the prostateAges 55–69 (earlier if higher risk)
Digital rectal examSize and shape of the prostateAs part of a regular checkup
Urine testSigns of infection or other problemsIf you have urinary symptoms

A high PSA level doesn't always mean cancer — it can also rise with an enlarged or inflamed prostate. Results must always be reviewed by a healthcare professional.

Daily Habits That Help

🥗

Eat More Plants

Fill your plate with vegetables, fruits, beans, and whole grains. Tomatoes, broccoli, and leafy greens are great choices.

🚶

Move Every Day

Aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week — like a brisk 30-minute walk, 5 days a week.

⚖️

Manage Your Weight

Extra weight, especially around the belly, is linked to more urinary symptoms and prostate problems.

☕

Limit Evening Drinks

Cut back on caffeine and alcohol, and drink less fluid 1–2 hours before bed to reduce nighttime bathroom trips.

🚻

Don't Hold It In

Go when you feel the urge, and take your time to empty your bladder fully.

🩺

Get Checked Regularly

Ask your doctor about prostate screening, especially if you're over 50 or have any of the risk factors above.

When to See a Doctor Right Away

  • You can't urinate at all
  • Blood in your urine or semen
  • Fever, chills, or pain along with urinary symptoms
  • Pain in your lower back, hips, or pelvis that doesn't go away

If you take prostate medication, never stop or change it without talking to your doctor first.
